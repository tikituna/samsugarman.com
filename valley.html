San Fernando Valley, California

The San Fernando Valley
Updated October 14, 2026

The San Fernando Valley is the flat part of Los Angeles north of the hills. About 1.8 million people live in it. The ridge road that divides it from the rest of the city is Mulholland Drive. The long road that runs the length of it is Ventura Boulevard. Burbank, where Mulholland, Inc. has its offices, is in the Valley's eastern end.

Points of interest, in no particular order.

  • Bob's Big Boy, Burbank. Open since 1949. The original Big Boy statue is out front.
  • Casa Vega, Sherman Oaks. Dark inside at noon. Open since 1956.
  • Art's Delicatessen, Studio City. Every sandwich is a work of Art, according to the sign.
  • Cupid's Hot Dogs, Northridge. A walk-up window, since 1946.
  • The Sepulveda Basin, Van Nuys. A flood-control reservoir that is a park nearly all of the time.
  • Ventura Boulevard between Laurel Canyon and Sepulveda, at night, with the windows down.
  • The Sherman Oaks Galleria, as it was in 1982, when a film crew used it as a high-school hangout.
  • The Valley Relics Museum, Van Nuys, which keeps the neon signs from the places that closed.
